Model Architecture

We employ a multimodal conditional diffusion policy that predicts a temporally coherent sequence of end-effector and gripper commands from visual, proprioceptive, and tactile observations. At each policy update, the model receives two consecutive observation frames. Each RGB wrist image is resized to 240 x 320 pixels, normalized using ImageNet statistics, and encoded by a ResNet-18 backbone trained from scratch. Batch-normalization layers in the backbone are replaced by group normalization to improve training stability with robotic datasets, and a random crop retaining 90% of the image extent is applied during training. The resulting 512-dimensional visual descriptor is concatenated directly with a 9-dimensional end-effector pose representation, consisting of 3D translation and a continuous 6D rotation representation, a scalar gripper-opening observation, and a 15-dimensional magnetic tactile feature vector. This produces a 537-dimensional multimodal feature for each observation frame. Features from the two observation frames are flattened into a 1,074-dimensional global conditioning vector.

The action generator is a conditional one-dimensional U-Net that denoises an action trajectory over a horizon of 16 control steps. Each action is 10-dimensional and contains 3D end-effector translation, a continuous 6D end-effector rotation, and one gripper command. End-effector actions are represented relative to the most recent observed TCP frame, whereas the gripper command remains an absolute opening value. The U-Net uses three feature scales with 512, 1,024, and 2,048 channels, residual 1D convolutional blocks with kernel size 5, eight-group normalization, Mish activations, and skip connections. A 128-dimensional sinusoidal diffusion-step embedding is processed by a multilayer perceptron and combined with the multimodal observation vector. This conditioning signal modulates every residual block through feature-wise affine transformations that predict both channel-wise scale and bias. The conditional diffusion network contains approximately 278.5 million parameters; together with the ResNet-18 observation encoder, the complete policy contains approximately 289.7 million trainable parameters.

Training follows an epsilon-prediction diffusion objective. Gaussian noise is added to normalized action trajectories at a randomly sampled diffusion timestep, and the network is optimized with mean-squared error to recover the injected noise. We use a DDIM scheduler with 100 diffusion steps, a squared-cosine beta schedule, beta endpoints of 1e-4 and 2e-2, and 100 denoising iterations during inference. The model is trained for 200 epochs with AdamW, a batch size of 64, an initial learning rate of 1e-4, cosine learning-rate decay, 500 warm-up steps, and an exponential moving average of the model parameters. During deployment, the generated trajectory is used in a receding-horizon manner, allowing the policy to repeatedly recondition future actions on newly acquired visual, robot-state, and tactile observations.

First-Frame Visual-Tactile Variant

We additionally use a first-frame visual-tactile variant designed for settings in which the scene appearance is static or changes too slowly to justify repeated image encoding. For each episode, the observation encoder receives the absolute first wrist-camera image as the visual context. During training, this episode-level image is inserted into the first visual slot of every sampled observation window, while all later visual slots are replaced by zeros. At inference time, the first image feature is encoded once after policy reset and cached for the remainder of the episode; subsequent camera frames are therefore excluded from the policy condition even though they may still be recorded for diagnostics. In contrast, the magnetic tactile embedding remains temporal and is encoded from the current observation at every policy update, allowing contact-dependent corrections to propagate through the observation sequence. A sequence-aware observation encoder constructs one feature vector per observation step, concatenating the cached first-frame visual feature at the initial slot with the current tactile features at all slots (and any explicitly enabled low-dimensional state features). The resulting sequence is flattened into the same global conditioning interface used by the conditional one-dimensional U-Net, so the diffusion action generator and receding-horizon execution procedure remain unchanged while the visual pathway becomes episode-level and the tactile pathway remains reactive.
